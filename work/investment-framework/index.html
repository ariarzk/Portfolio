BT-01/Governance/Case study

Investment Framework & Governance

A standard way to evaluate, approve and review capital — where none existed.

Role

Corporate Development & Finance

Domain

Capital allocation · Corporate governance

Period

2024 — in force

Instrument

Board decree · 2024

01

Situation

The business was chronically underinvested. Capital decisions were taken case by case: no standard for how an opportunity was justified, no stated limit on who could commit the company, and no obligation to come back afterwards and say what the money had bought.

The consequence was not only slow decisions. Without a common basis of comparison, two proposals could not be ranked against each other, and a proposal could not be ranked against doing nothing.

02

Challenge

The framework had to hold two audiences at once. It needed to be formal enough that a director and a commissioner could rely on it when the capital structure was at stake, and light enough that a plant manager could actually produce a proposal against it without a corporate finance function to help.

It also had to work in a business with no history of structured appraisal: the framework itself would be the first thing that taught the organisation what a case for capital looks like.

03

Approach

Written as a board decree rather than a guideline, and issued under the company’s own good-governance article — so the rules on transparency, accountability and fairness the company already claimed were the rules the investment process now had to meet. Drafted in the Corporate Development seat and signed off at director and commissioner level.

The policy sets five things and refuses to leave any of them to custom: what the company may invest in, who decides at each level, what a proposal must contain, how a proposal is assessed, and what is owed back after the money is spent. A glossary sits at the front for a reason — the terms it defines are the ones the organisation had been using loosely.

Two permitted classes: fixed assets — modernisation, expansion, rejuvenation — and project investment, from new sites and products to distribution infrastructure, process digitalisation and environmental capacity. Anything outside them needs the policy changed, not bypassed.

04

Execution

The proposal document — a business requirement document — carries the need, the cost–benefit estimate, the risks and their mitigations, the implementation timeline and the score. It is drafted by a manager or senior manager, or by a head under supervision, which puts the case in the hands of the people who will have to run the asset.

Above it sits an investment committee the VP convenes, with an independent advisor whose job is expert judgement on the object itself — for a machine, its performance, maintenance cost and fit with the line. Appraisal runs across five dimensions: strategic alignment to the long-range plan, cost efficiency, operational impact and capacity, demand fulfilment, product quality, each with operational and technology risk assessed alongside.

Decision rights are explicit and bounded. The CEO approves every investment; the commissioner approves only what changes the capital structure — bank debt secured on company assets — and reviews its effect on leverage and solvency. Board-level cases travel as an investment memorandum: thesis, business plan, structure, risk analysis, funding structure and financials. Every stage carries its own service-level clock, so a proposal cannot die by sitting.

05

Result

A company-wide framework covering the full investment lifecycle, from the first written case to the accountability report after commissioning — in force since December 2024 and reviewed periodically rather than left to age.

  • Evaluation and approval standardised: the same criteria, the same scoring instrument and the same gates for every proposal.
  • Decision rights stated in writing, including the single case that must reach the commissioner.
  • Management visibility into rationale, expected return, risk and — through the accountability report — what the investment actually delivered.
  • Its first major test followed under the same rules: BT-02, the blowing machine investment.

The view

A policy is only governance if something is owed back.

Approval limits are the easy half. The half that changes behaviour is the accountability report — the obligation to return, after commissioning, and state what the investment delivered against the case that won it the money.
