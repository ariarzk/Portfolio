BT-02/Investment/Case study

Blowing Machine Investment

The framework’s first major test — capacity for growth, justified and financed.

Role

Corporate Development & Finance

Domain

Manufacturing capacity · Capital investment

Period

Nov 2023 — Sep 2024

Type

Capex · debt-financed

01

Situation

Manufacturing was running out of room. Two ageing two-cavity blowing machines carried the bottle supply across three shifts, and they could not keep up: just under a third of output was being toll-manufactured outside the plant, at a cost per unit the company did not control and a quality it could only check after delivery.

Capacity was the limit on growth, and it cost money every month it went unaddressed.

02

Challenge

This was the first major investment to run under the governance framework written the year before, so it tested two things at once: whether the plant could add capacity, and whether the new process could handle a decision that size.

It also had to be financed. The company needed a bank facility secured on its own assets — the one class of investment the policy sends all the way to the commissioner — so the case had to hold up for a lender as well as for the board.

03

Approach

The case was built the way the policy requires: a business requirement document setting out the need, the cost–benefit estimate, the risks and their mitigations and the implementation timeline, then an investment memorandum for the board — thesis, business plan, funding structure, risk analysis and financials.

Appraisal was deliberately conservative. Cash flows were discounted against the company’s required return, and the case was carried on cost savings the plant could name — labour, maintenance, materials and the toll-manufacturing bill that would disappear — rather than on expected demand growth. That is why the approved return was only 4.35% — the upside was deliberately left out.

The scoring sheet was completed independently by three assessors at board level — the commissioner, the chief executive, and audit and compliance — across the six criteria the policy defines. Financing was structured alongside the appraisal, so the funding request and the business case reached the commissioner as one document.

04

Execution

One four-cavity machine replaced the pair it was bought to retire. Output that had been going out to toll manufacturers came back in-house, and the line covered demand on two shifts where the old configuration had needed three.

Cost moved in four directions, and the report records all four: labour and maintenance fell sharply, the toll bill and the outsourced material cost went to zero, and electricity rose — a faster machine draws more power. Netted at the demand level the report uses, the plant’s operating cost for the same output fell by roughly seven-eighths.

Demand then ran about 50% above the forecast the case was built on, which explains most of the gap between the approved return and the realised one.

05

Result

Delivered at a tenth of a per cent over the estimate, financed exactly as structured, and reported back to the board and commissioners in a formal accountability report eleven months after the first works began.

  • Manufacturing capacity up approximately 4×, on fewer shifts.
  • Fixed labour requirement down by roughly 20 crew — about IDR 70 million a month.
  • Supporting packaging material down about IDR 3,600 per box — roughly IDR 150 million a month.
  • Toll manufacturing eliminated: output that had been made outside came back into the plant.
  • IRR 38.35% against 4.35% approved; payback three years against six; benefit–cost 1.42 against 0.23.
  • The first major investment delivered under the new governance framework — and the first to be closed out under it.

The view

The case was approved on savings, not on growth.

It was built on savings the plant could name rather than on expected demand growth, which is why the approved return was low and the realised one was not. The accountability report states this, along with the two months lost and the one cost line that rose.
